Physical quantum bits (qubits) are intrinsically vulnerable to environmental perturbations — acoustic phonons, stray magnetic fields, and cosmic background radiation will corrupt fragile quantum superposition states within microseconds.

In our latest research published in *Physical Review Applied*, we demonstrate a crucial milestone: **a distance-5 surface code with 72 physical qubits outperforms a distance-3 surface code with 17 physical qubits on the identical dilution refrigerator architecture**.

### Why This Represents a Watershed Moment
In classical digital computers, increasing the transistor count does not make memory less reliable. In quantum computing, however, scaling the number of noisy physical qubits previously compounded the noise.

By confirming that error-correcting syndrome cycles detect and mitigate errors faster than additional physical qubits introduce them, our team has proven that **fault-tolerant quantum computation is an achievable engineering reality**.
