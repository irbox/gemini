Major milestone in quantum error correction: Scaling logical qubits

Google Quantum AI researchers demonstrate a surface code that reduces error rates as system size increases, clearing a fundamental hurdle toward practical quantum computers.

Demis Hassabis
CEO of Google DeepMind
October 28, 2026 • 5 min

For quantum computers to solve real-world problems in materials science, battery chemistry, and cryptographic verification, they must be capable of error-corrected fault-tolerant computation.

In a new paper published in Nature, the Google Quantum AI team presents experimental results demonstrating that a larger 72-qubit surface code achieves a lower logical error rate than a smaller 17-qubit surface code on the same superconducting quantum processor.

Why This Matters

In conventional computing, adding more transistors does not increase the error rate. In quantum computing, however, physical qubits are extremely sensitive to thermal fluctuations and cosmic radiation.

Until now, making a quantum system bigger typically made it noisier. By crossing the threshold where quantum error correction outpaces the introduction of physical noise, our team has proven that fault-tolerant quantum computing is physically viable.
